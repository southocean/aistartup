# AI SEO audit, first run: 5 Stockholm small businesses (13 Nov 2026)

What we did: asked ChatGPT (guest), Perplexity (guest) and Gemini (Nam's account, Pro) the same 10
Swedish buyer questions across 5 trades (Vietnamese restaurant, video production, hairdresser, plumber,
accounting), then ran a brand check ("what do you know about X?") on one small business per trade and
checked the answers against the business's own website. Raw answers: `ai-seo-audit-raw.md`.

The five brand-checked businesses are anonymized (Restaurant A, Video studio B, Salon C, Plumber D,
Accounting firm E) because this repo is public. Other names are only what the engines recommended.

## What we found

**1. The engines don't agree, so "rank #1 in AI" isn't a thing.**
"Best hairdresser on Södermalm" got 10 different salons from three engines with zero overlap.
Accounting firms: ChatGPT and Gemini shared one name (REAK) out of eight. Only two businesses were named
by all three engines: Josef Runsten (wedding films) and Klara Färdiga Film (ad films).
A useful audit has to cover several engines and report *frequency*, not a rank.

**2. Publishing a price is the strongest lever we saw.**
Firms with a fixed price on their own site were quoted with it, line by line, and often put first:
Plumber D, Video studio B, Lilla Ekonomibyrån (390 kr/mån),
Dryft and Jour Eliten (Perplexity's only two plumbers, both with price pages). Perplexity put Midnatt Media first
"because they're transparent with start prices". Price questions are what buyers ask, and the engines reach for
whoever answers them.

**3. A thin website makes the AI answer thin or inconsistent.**
Salon C (top Google rating, 100+ reviews) has no prices or service list on its own site. ChatGPT told the
reader it couldn't find a price list, mentioned old "spa treatments", and said to phone. Gemini instead gave
a full price list from a source it didn't show. Same salon, two different pictures, neither from the owner.

**4. The AI also gets things wrong when the site is clear.**
Accounting firm E's site shows prices by turnover band. ChatGPT quoted the numbers but
said it was unclear what separates the tiers; Gemini got it right. ChatGPT also flagged two different
addresses (office vs registered) and volunteered the firm's latest revenue and a loss, which the owner
would probably rather not see in a sales answer.

**5. ChatGPT leans on Google-Maps-style data.**
It shows rating, review count, address and phone cards. Review count and rating clearly feed who gets
listed. Perplexity leaned on own websites, TheFork, Reco, Wanderlog and Reddit. Gemini mixed both.

**6. When the site is good, the answer is good.**
Restaurant A (menu and hours online), Video studio B and Plumber D got rich, accurate answers that
matched their own websites.

## What this means for the idea

- There *is* something concrete to sell: a one-page report per business showing (a) whether it's named for
  its buyer questions in 3 engines, (b) what the engines say about it and where that's wrong, (c) 3–5 fixes.
  Every business in this run had at least one finding an owner would care about.
- The fixes so far are boring and cheap: publish prices and a service list, keep Google Business Profile
  and review sites consistent, fix conflicting addresses. That's honest advice, but it suggests the value is
  in the audit and the nudge, not secret GEO tactics. Course material would mostly be "make your site answer
  the questions customers ask".
- Doing it by hand took about 2 hours for 5 businesses × 3 engines, mostly waiting for Gemini. Scriptable.

## Limits of this run

- One run per question. Answers vary between runs, accounts and locations; we didn't measure that.
- ChatGPT and Perplexity were guest sessions; Perplexity hit its guest limit on one question.
  Gemini was Nam's own account (location shown as Uppsala), so it may be personalised.
- Brand checks done in ChatGPT for all five, Gemini for two. Not in Perplexity.
- The businesses were picked by us from the answers, not by asking owners. No owner has seen a report yet.
